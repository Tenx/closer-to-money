# First Customer Playbook

Getting from 0 → 1 paying customer. Organized by vertical.
The first customer is always manual. Always uncomfortable. Always worth it.

---

## Universal rules (applies to all verticals)

1. **The first customer comes from your network, not from the internet.** Cold outbound to strangers is for companies that already have proof.
2. **You don't find the customer — you identify them.** Who do you already know that has this problem?
3. **Charge from day 1.** Even $10. Free users give you feedback; paying users give you a business.
4. **The first ask is not "buy my product." It's "tell me about your problem."** Then sell after you understand.
5. **Rejection data > no data.** 10 nos tells you more than 10 months of building.

## Vertical: SaaS / Dev Tools

**Who buys first:** A founder, team lead, or senior IC who experiences the pain daily. Not an enterprise procurement team.

**Where to find them:**
- LinkedIn: search for job title + company size (10–200 people is the sweet spot)
- Twitter/X: search for complaints about the problem your tool solves ("hate dealing with X", "looking for a tool that does Y")
- Indie Hackers, Hacker News (who is it for section), Reddit r/entrepreneur r/startups
- Slack communities for your target vertical (SaaS founders, devs, etc.)

**Outreach script:**
> "Hey [name], I saw your post about [problem]. I'm building [tool] that handles this. Would you be open to a 20-min call? I'll do the setup for you and won't charge for the first month."

**First 10 customers path:**
1. Week 1: Find 20 people via LinkedIn/Twitter/community. DM all 20.
2. Expect 5 responses, 2 calls, 1 paying customer.
3. Ask each paying customer for 2 referrals.
4. Use testimonials from customer 1 to close customers 2–5.

**Pricing for first customer:** Charge 50% of your intended price. Explicitly tell them they're getting founder pricing in exchange for honest feedback.

## Vertical: Content / Newsletter / Course

**Who buys first:** Someone who already consumes your free content. First paid customer is almost always a current subscriber or follower.

**Where to find them:**
- Your existing email list (even if tiny)
- People who replied to your tweets/posts with substantive comments
- People who shared your content with a personal note

**The 1-1-1 launch:**
1. Email 1 person today: "I'm running a beta cohort of [course/community]. $[price]. 5 spots. Want in?"
2. Do the first cohort live, not pre-recorded. The live delivery IS the product development.
3. Charge for the second cohort at full price, use recording/transcript from first.

**Pricing for first customer:** Founding member pricing. 40–50% discount, never expires, they feel invested in your success.

**Common mistake:** Waiting until the course is "done" to sell. Sell the outline, then build it with the first cohort.

## Vertical: Ecommerce / Physical Goods

**Who buys first:** Someone in your immediate social circle, or someone in a community passionate about the niche.

**Where to find them:**
- Instagram/TikTok comments on accounts in your niche
- Facebook Groups for the target hobby/interest
- Reddit communities (r/handmade, r/[niche], etc.)
- Etsy (if listing already there) — optimize for search visibility

**First 10 sales path:**
1. List on Etsy/Gumroad/Shopify with real photos (no mockups).
2. Post in 3 relevant communities: "I made this, here's the story behind it." No hard sell.
3. DM everyone who comments positively with a direct link to purchase.
4. For physical goods: offer to ship for free to first 5 customers in exchange for a photo review.

**Pricing for first customer:** Never discount for ecommerce — it trains buyers to wait for sales. Use free shipping or a bonus item instead.

**Common mistake:** Trying to build a brand before making a sale. First sale > first logo.

## Vertical: Consulting / Freelance / Agency

**Who buys first:** Someone you already know who has the budget and the problem. This is the fastest path and most people skip it.

**The only prospecting you need for first client:**
1. Write down 20 people you know who might have this problem (or know someone who does).
2. Send them a personal note: "I'm starting [service]. I'm looking for 2 clients to work with closely. You came to mind because [specific reason]. Do you have 15 min this week?"
3. Repeat until you have your first call.

**On the first call:**
- Listen 80%, talk 20%.
- Ask: "What does success look like in 90 days?"
- At the end: "Here's what I'd do. My fee is $[X]. Want to start Monday?"

**Pricing for first client:** Do NOT undercharge (signals low quality). Do NOT overcharge (signals risk). Charge 70% of your target rate. Communicate: "I'm taking on 2 clients at a reduced rate while I build out my process. After that it's $[full rate]."

**Common mistake:** Writing a long proposal before confirming the client wants to hire you. Confirm interest verbally first, THEN write the proposal.

## Vertical: Side Project / Indie App / Utility Tool

**Who buys first:** Someone who found you through Product Hunt, indie maker communities, or niche forums. Or a friend who has the exact problem.

**Where to find them:**
- Product Hunt launch (even a quiet launch gets early adopters)
- Hacker News "Show HN" — include price in the post, never apologize for charging
- Reddit communities for the problem space
- AppSumo (if one-time purchase) — takes 70% but gives you volume and reviews
- Twitter: post about what you built, tag people who might care

**Launch checklist for first paying customer:**
1. Add a payment button BEFORE you think you're ready.
2. Write a landing page that answers: "What does this do? Who is it for? How much? What's the risk if it doesn't work out?"
3. Have a refund policy (reduces purchase anxiety, rarely used).
4. Post to 3 communities in one day. Monitor for 48 hours. Respond to every comment.

**Pricing for first customer:** Pick a price that feels slightly uncomfortable (too high). You can always go down; going up is harder.

**Common mistake:** Launching to zero audience. Before you launch, spend 2 weeks being helpful in the communities where your buyers are.
